# Stourify 0.10.0

## You decide whether your spots show where they are

**Settings → Privacy** has a new switch, **Show location on spots**, right under Private account.
Turn it off and the map position of the spots you share stops being sent to anyone else.

Two things are worth knowing before you flip it, and the screen says both:

- It works from now on. A position someone's phone already downloaded cannot be called back.
- Your spots drop out of "nearby" results, because nobody can find them by being close to them any
  more. They still show up in Discover, in search, on your profile and through a direct link.

## A spot with no location still gets its page

When a spot's contributor has hidden its location, the spot keeps its card in every list and loses
only its pin on the map. Its page says **Location not shown** instead of leaving an empty space that
looks like something failed to load.

## Links to a spot open the spot

A `stourify://` link to a spot now opens that spot inside the app: its page, its About tab, its
photos or its reviews. Pressing Back takes you to Discover, not to an empty screen.

## Your privacy switches stay where you put them

Fixed: turning off a privacy switch could quietly turn itself back on a little later, after the app
synced in the background. The switches in Settings → Privacy are now changed only by you, from that
screen.

## When the feed says no, it tells you why

If your account is not allowed to see the feed, the app now says which of two things is going on:
either your account was never added to Stourify's community (sign out and back in, and ask us if it
keeps happening), or it was added but is not allowed to see those posts. Neither message blames your
connection, because neither has anything to do with it.

## Notes

- The version at the bottom of the sign-in and Settings screens should read **0.10.0**.
- Nothing you have saved on the phone is affected by this update, including anything created while
  you were offline and waiting to send.
- Part of this release is work you cannot see: stronger checks that stop a public build of the app
  from ever being pointed at the wrong server.
